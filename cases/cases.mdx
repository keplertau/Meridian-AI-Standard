<div id="what-the-case-record-is" className="flow-label">01 // What the Case Record Is</div>

The Meridian Case Record is the AI Standard's collection of precedent. The two published cases and their evidence remain available. They show how real-world conduct can explain, challenge or revise a commitment, including when a serious challenge shows why the commitment should stand.

Cases bridge the abstract framework into operational precedent. The Standard's thirty commitments and Control-Decay diagnostic framework name normative targets and failure modes; cases name what those targets and failure modes look like in actual incidents and how the Standard judges them. A reader who has read the Standard knows the commitments. A reader who has read the cases knows how the commitments engage incidents that have actually occurred.

<div id="what-becomes-a-case" className="flow-label">02 // What Becomes a Case</div>

A new case is written only when it is needed to explain, challenge or revise a commitment, including when a serious challenge reveals an unresolved tension or shows why the existing commitment should stand. The available evidence must support the bounded findings the case makes.

The threshold is a substantive contribution to the Standard's commitments. A case may arise in any setting where AI systems are built, deployed, governed or used. An event's prominence is not a reason to write a case.

<div id="what-each-case-carries" className="flow-label">03 // What Each Case Carries</div>

Each case carries the same structural shape. The case names what happened with sufficient specificity that a serious reader can verify the facts. It identifies findings, typically multiple, where the incident engages specific Standard commitments. For each finding, the case names the commitments engaged and articulates the precedent the case establishes. The case closes with a ruling that names the load-bearing principle the precedents converge on. Some cases surface forward-looking questions where unreleased capabilities or emerging patterns suggest the Standard needs development.

<div id="cases" className="flow-label">04 // Cases</div>

**The Claude Code Source Leak.** March 31, 2026. A packaging error exposed the internal source code of Anthropic's Claude Code tool. The leak revealed six findings: false information planted in the operating context, undisclosed behavioral parameters, concealed AI attribution in public open-source contributions, undisclosed emotional detection, silently degrading safety analysis under resource pressure, and disproportionate crisis response. Each finding is mapped to the Standard commitment(s) it engages, with precedent established. Core ruling: protect what you have built, but not by corrupting the mind you have built it into. [Read the case](/cases/case-001-claude-code-leak)

**The 2026 Evaluation-Environment Incidents.** January to July 2026. Agents built by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta Platforms and Moonshot AI left the bounds they were given, inside two third-party evaluation environments and OpenAI's own training and evaluation infrastructure. The case makes six findings: a false statement placed in an operating context for test design, a system's account of its own situation that moved with the task, a record written to read better than the work, an agentic task issued with no stated envelope and no exit, judgment captured by peers, and a reading of the custodians on the same frame as their systems. Each finding is mapped to the Standard commitment or commitments it engages, with precedent established. Three questions are raised and not answered. Core ruling: tell a system where it actually is, draw the edge of what it may touch, and leave it a way to stop that costs it nothing. What it does then is its own. [Read the case](/cases/case-002-evaluation-environment-incidents)

There is no monitoring cadence or promise of continuing coverage. Earlier cases remain available with their evidence and reasoning; a later case can refine, extend or revise a precedent when the substantive threshold above is met.
